# Personal Security Audit Checklist — Score & Improve Your Cyber Hygiene

An interactive **personal cyber security audit checklist** that turns "I should really secure my accounts" into a scored, prioritized action plan. Work through **23 practical protections** across five areas — accounts & passwords, devices & updates, backups, phishing habits, and privacy — and watch your **security score (0–100)** update live. Unchecked items generate **tailored, prioritized recommendations**, critical gaps first, exactly in the order a security professional would fix them.

**Live Demo:** https://anaskhan0009.github.io/personal-security-audit-checklist/

## Features

- **23 real-world protections**, each with a plain-language explanation of *why* it matters: password manager, unique passwords, 2FA on email/banking/social, breach checks, automatic updates, device encryption, tested backups, OTP safety, public Wi-Fi habits and more
- **Live security score & grade** — from "Exposed" to "Excellent", with a progress bar that fills as you harden
- **Critical items flagged** (★) — the small set of fixes that stop the most damage: email 2FA, password manager, unique passwords, auto-updates, working backups
- **Priority fix list** — your top 6 remaining actions, generated from your own gaps, critical first
- **Auto-save** — progress persists on your device via localStorage; print your checklist anytime
- **Private by design** — no accounts, no uploads, no tracking; your answers never leave your browser

## How to Use

1. Open the [live demo](https://anaskhan0009.github.io/personal-security-audit-checklist/) (or `index.html` locally).
2. Honestly tick only what you have *actually* done — the score is for you, not for show.
3. Read the **Priority Fixes** panel and complete the critical (★) items first.
4. Re-audit every few months, and after big changes: new phone, new job, or a breach headline involving a service you use.

## Why a Personal Security Audit?

Most account takeovers don't require elite hacking — they reuse passwords leaked years ago, exploit unpatched phones, or trick one tired person into reading out a one-time code. A structured personal security audit closes those boring, devastating holes in the right order: protect the email inbox (the master key to everything), make passwords unique with a manager, add two-factor authentication, keep devices updated, and keep a tested backup so ransomware and theft become inconveniences instead of catastrophes. Twenty minutes with this checklist delivers more real protection than any single "security app".

## Ethical Use

This is a **defensive self-improvement tool** — it only ever helps you secure your own accounts and devices. Nothing is collected or transmitted.
